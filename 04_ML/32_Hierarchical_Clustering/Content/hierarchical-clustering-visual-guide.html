Distance is the shared starting idea

Both methods need a way to decide what is near. For two-dimensional points, we use straight-line Euclidean distance.

d(A,B) = √[(xA − xB)² + (yA − yB)²]

Meaning: a smaller distance means the two observations are more similar according to these features.

What is K-means, and why is it here?

Tab 1 gave us ten unlabeled points. We now need an algorithm that can divide them into sensible groups. K-means is the first clustering method used in notebook 12.

First understand the name

K = how many groups we wantIn this lesson, K = 2, so we ask the algorithm to find exactly two clusters.
Means = averagesEach group is represented by its average position, called a centroid or center.

K-means means: create K groups by repeatedly moving K average points. It does not know the correct groups in advance.

The complete K-means story

Choose KWe choose two groups because the scatter plot appears to have two broad regions.
Place centersPut one temporary center inside the data for each requested group.
Assign and moveSend each point to its nearest center, then move each center to its group average.
Stop when stableRepeat until no point changes group and the centers no longer move.

What is hierarchical clustering?

It is a different way to group the same ten points. Unlike K-means, it does not move centers. It builds larger groups by joining smaller groups.

Think of building a family tree from the bottom upward

At the start, A, B, C, and every other point is a separate one-person group. The closest two groups join. That new group can then join another point or group. Every join becomes one branch in a tree.

Hierarchical = arranged in levelsSmall groups sit at lower levels. Larger combined groups sit at higher levels.
Agglomerative = join from the bottomThis is the version used here: start with individuals and repeatedly combine them.

The complete hierarchical story

Start with 10 groupsEvery point is temporarily its own cluster.
Compare groupsUse a linkage rule to calculate the distance between every possible pair.
Merge the nearestJoin only the pair with the smallest linkage value.
Save the historyRepeat until one group remains. Draw every join as a dendrogram branch.

Easy counting rule: with 10 points, clusters remaining = 10 − completed merges. For example, after 3 merges there are 7 clusters.

How to read the dendrogram on the right

Branch heightHigher means the groups were more different.

Now compare the two methods

They solve the same general clustering problem, but their mechanics and outputs are different.

QuestionK-meansHierarchical clustering
What moves?Centroids move toward the mean.Nothing in data space; groups merge.
Choose cluster count first?Yes. Set K before fitting.Not necessarily. Inspect and cut the dendrogram later.
Main calculationPoint-to-centroid distance.Cluster-to-cluster linkage distance.
Main outputLabels and centroid positions.Labels plus a merge tree.
Best visual ideaCenters move; assignments repeat.Branches join from bottom to top.
Curved moon-shaped dataOften splits it poorly because centers favor compact groups.Single linkage can follow connected curved shapes, as notebook 12 demonstrates.
Use K-means whenyou expect compact groups, know K, and need speed on larger data.
Use hierarchical whenyou want the grouping history or do not yet know the best cut.
Before either methodscale features, inspect outliers, and decide whether Euclidean distance makes sense.
Final memory: clustering is the task; K-means and hierarchical clustering are different algorithms for completing that task.
