# Channel phrase bank — the desi-teacher feel in clear English (creator, 2026-10-02)

Every script uses these conversational teaching phrases to *guide* the viewer, like a teacher with a student.
The feel comes from **delivery and rhythm**, not slang: no "na", "yaar", etc.

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Get attention | "See…", "Now, listen…", "Look at this…" |
| Introduce an idea | "Let's say…", "Suppose…", "Imagine this…" |
| Explain simply | "Basically…", "In simple words…", "What this means is…" |
| Emphasize a point | "The main thing is…", "Remember this…", "Here's the catch…" |
| Ask the viewer to think | "Think about it…", "What happens now?", "Why do we need this?" |
| Check understanding | "Right?", "Makes sense?", "Got it?" |
| Clear up confusion | "Now, don't get confused…", "Let me explain…", "Let's break it down…" |
| Move forward | "Okay, now…", "So, next…", "Coming to the next point…" |
| Summarize | "So, basically…", "That's the idea…", "Simple, right?" |

**Core five for technical videos:** "See", "Let's say", "Now", "Right?", "Here's the catch".

Rules:
- Use a phrase only where it does a job (open a part, introduce the example, land the key point, check, move on).
  Not one per sentence. Roughly 1–2 per part.
- Vary them across parts and across episodes; don't repeat the same opener in consecutive parts.
- "Like" only for a real example or comparison ("like a sticker"). Never as filler.
- Flowing sentences with connectors (so, because, and that's why), written as paragraphs.

Model example (creator's):
> See, let's say you want to predict a house price. You give the model examples of houses and their prices.
> Now, it learns patterns from those examples. Simple, right? But here's the catch: if your examples are poor,
> its predictions can be poor too.

## Indian-classroom extras (creator asked for "more Indian teacher style", 2026-10-03)
Indian-English rhythm teachers really use; 1 per part, where it fits. The accent is his natural voice; never fake one.
- "isn't it?" · "Just think once…" · "I'll tell you one thing…" · "First of all…" · "First things first…"
- "Understood?" · "No problem…" · "Very simple only." · "we only" · "…itself" · "…also" (end of sentence) · "…myself"
Model (Intro v4, `episodes/day_00/reading_sheet_v4.md`): "Now, just think once. Who connects those models to the
app? Us. Yes, we only! Our Java services send them data… So ML is becoming part of our job itself."
