The board

27 tickets, one sitting each.

Roughly 68 hours, about seven weeks at three or four sittings a week. A ticket is not finished when the code runs — it is finished when its explain-back questions are answered in your own words, and every acceptance criterion has been re-run, not re-read.

How one ticket runs

1

Open it

Read Story, Background, Steps. Set status: doing.

2

Write it

You write every line. Stuck? hint → direction → partial example. Never the answer first.

3

Hand it over

Set status: review, answer the explain-back questions, say “verify DAF-07”.

4

Verified

Every criterion is run. Pass → done, next ticket. Fail → same ticket, named criterion.

Decisions on the record

Every real choice becomes a file in docs/decisions/, so that in three months the reason is still on disk. Four are accepted; the fifth is the one the data is now asking for.

accepted D-001 · DAF-02

Predict the number, then apply the AQI table

Chosen: PM2.5 in µg/m³, with the CPCB table read off it afterwards.

Why: a category-only model makes 91 and 400 the same answer. The number keeps the severity and still gives the yes or no.

accepted D-002 · DAF-02

Tomorrow's 24-hour mean, 00:00–23:59 IST

Chosen: the full day, not the 07:00–10:00 assembly window.

Why: the CPCB bands are defined on the full day, so the number means the same thing to everyone. A morning target can come later.

accepted D-003 · DAF-03 · 16 Sep

Keep stations with 730+ days of real coverage

Chosen: coverage_days = last − first, keep if ≥ 730. 50 stations pass.

Why: objective, easy to explain, and based on elapsed time rather than calendar years.

The record says it plainly: a minimum quality gate, not the final word. It also says the project should then prefer the newest 3–4 years — which is what D-004 picks up.

accepted D-004 · DAF-04 · 16 Sep

Train on the most recent 3–4 years

Chosen: the latest four years inside the valid pool — 2022-09-16 → 2026-09-16. 44 of the 50 survive.

Why: Delhi's traffic, fuel and policy have changed. A model for tomorrow should learn the current regime, not 2016.

Filed under ticket DAF-04, though DAF-04 on the board is the S3 collector. Worth a look when the ticket opens.

open D-005

What to do about the re-issued CPCB sensors

The problem: D-003 rewards a long history and D-004 wants a recent one. OpenAQ re-issued the CPCB feed in late 2022, so at most Delhi stations there are two sensor records — a retired one with a long span, and a live one with a short span. The 730-day gate keeps the retired one and throws away the live one, at 37 of the 44 selected stations.

The choice: measure coverage inside the window instead of overall, or stitch the old and new sensor at a station into one series, or lower the gate for sensors that are still reporting. The evidence is on the daily-table page →
