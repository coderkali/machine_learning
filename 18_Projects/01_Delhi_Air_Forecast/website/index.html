Asha has to decide tonight whether 600 children
stand outside tomorrow morning.

She checks today's air quality and assumes tomorrow is the same. That is right on most days and wrong on exactly the days that matter — the evening the wind drops, the week the crop smoke arrives, the morning after Diwali. This project builds the thing that tells her tomorrow's number, tonight.

Next thing to do — DAF-04, the collector

D-003 and D-004 are written and the first daily table exists. But that table came out of a notebook calling the API, and DAF-04 asks for something different: a repeatable script that pulls the original OpenAQ S3 files into data/raw/openaq/ and never edits them.

1Read the station ids from data/raw/stations.csv
2Download the original archive files — no reshaping, no cleaning, raw as they arrived
3Skip what is already on disk, so a second run costs nothing
4Count missing station-days without crashing on them
5Print a summary: downloaded, skipped, missing, stored

Start with one station for one month. Only when that works, collect the rest.

Before the collector runs, one thing needs deciding. D-003 keeps stations by how long they ran; D-004 wants data that is recent. Those two pull in opposite directions, and at 37 of the 44 selected stations the pair has picked a sensor that stopped reporting in October 2022 over its live replacement. The collector will faithfully download whichever list it is given — so the list wants fixing first. The whole finding is here →
The board is behind the work. DAF-03's front matter still reads todo although D-003, D-004 and both data files exist, and DAF-02 still reads review. The board on this site shows what the files say.
