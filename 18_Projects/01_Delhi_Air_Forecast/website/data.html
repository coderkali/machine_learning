Station discovery · notebook 03

Delhi has 102 air monitors on OpenAQ.
Only 50 can teach a model anything.

Before any measurement is downloaded, one question has to be answered: which monitors have enough history to be worth downloading? This page is what came back, and what was thrown away.

From 102 locations down to 50 usable stations

Each step here is a real filter in the notebook, not a rounding.

The trap on step one. The first request came back with exactly 100 locations — the page limit — and the metadata said there were more. Stopping there would have silently dropped two stations. The loop asks for page 2, page 3, and so on until a page returns fewer than 100.

Who runs the monitors

CPCB — India's Central Pollution Control Board — is the backbone: 106 of the 147 sensor records, and 42 of the 50 that qualify. The rest are state boards and a handful of low-cost sensors.

147 sensors, but only 102 locations. A station can hold more than one PM2.5 sensor — an old one that was retired and a new one that replaced it. Choosing one sensor per station, or stitching them, is part of what D-003 has to decide.

How much history each sensor really has

This is the check that was got wrong first, and then corrected.

clears the 730-day rule too short
the wrong way

Counting calendar years

The first version asked whether a sensor's first and last reading fell in different years.

A sensor running from 31 Dec 2025 to 1 Jan 2026 touches two calendar years and would have passed — on one day of data.

the fix

Counting elapsed days

coverage_days = (last_reading − first_reading).days, and keep it if it is 730 or more.

That measures the actual span. 50 sensors pass — at 50 distinct locations, so no station is double-counted.

What this check still does not prove. A span from 2016 to 2026 says the sensor reported at both ends. It says nothing about the gaps in between — dead months, missing hours, a stretch of zeros. That is DAF-07's job, and it is the reason the raw hourly data gets downloaded rather than the tidy /days aggregates.
