Why 42 stations only gave six weeks each

This is the finding, and it is not a mistake in the notebook. It is a trap in the source data.

OpenAQ re-issued the CPCB feed in late 2022. The old sensor ids stop dead on 31 October 2022 — 19 of them share the identical final timestamp — and new sensor ids appear for the same physical stations in February 2025.

D-003 measures a sensor's total span. The retired sensor ran 2018 → 2022, a span of 1,696 days, so it passes the 730-day gate comfortably. Its live replacement has run 569 days so far, so it fails. The rule keeps the dead one.

569

Neither rule is wrong on its own. A 730-day minimum is a reasonable quality gate, and a four-year window is a reasonable recency rule. They only fail together, because span and recency are different things and the CPCB re-issue splits one station's history across two sensor ids. This is what D-005 has to settle, and three fixes are available: measure coverage inside the window rather than overall; stitch the old and new sensor at a location into one series; or waive the gate for sensors still reporting today.

What the readings themselves look like

Thin as the table is, the values in it are real Delhi air — and they land where you would expect.

899 of the 3,342 readings — 26.9% — are Poor or worse. That is the number to remember when the metric is chosen: a model that simply never predicts a bad day would still be right about three times in four. Accuracy will look fine. Recall will not.

The season that makes this hard

Mean of the cached readings by month. Bars above the dashed line are months where the average day already sends children indoors.

November averages 229 µg/m³ and July averages 32 — a seven-fold swing inside one year. Two consequences follow. A model that does not know the month will be badly wrong twice a year. And a train/test split that cuts across seasons rather than along time will quietly let the model see the winter it is about to be tested on — which is what DAF-12 exists to prevent.

Read these bars with care. September and October carry 740 and 825 readings; February has 136. The monthly means are not equally trustworthy, and they come from the same lopsided table as everything else on this page.
