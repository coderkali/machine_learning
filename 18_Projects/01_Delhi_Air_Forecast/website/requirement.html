What we promised to build

Predict tomorrow's number, tonight —
and beat the free alternative.

An ordinary requirement says what a system does. A machine-learning requirement has to say something extra: how wrong it is allowed to be, and compared to what. A model is never right or wrong, only better or worse than the thing you would have done anyway.

The line that decides everything

India's CPCB turns a PM2.5 concentration into six named bands. Asha's whole decision sits on one boundary.

Two things follow from this picture. Delhi in winter lives in the right-hand bands, so "unhealthy" is not a rare event — which changes what a good score looks like. And an error of 10 µg/m³ at a true value of 40 costs nothing, while the same error at a true value of 85 flips Asha's answer. The metric has to know that.

The 18:00 rule — the one that kills models in production

The forecast is made at 18:00 IST today. Training may only use what genuinely exists at that moment.

Today, hour by hour

  • 00:00 – 17:00
    Today's readings arrive, hour by hour. These are usable.
  • 18:00 IST
    The prediction is made here. Everything after this line is the future.
  • 18:00 – 23:59
    Today's remaining hours. Not knowable — so today's own 24-hour mean is not a feature.
  • Tomorrow 00:00 – 23:59
    The target. Tomorrow's forecast weather may be used; tomorrow's actual weather may not.

Knowable at 18:00?

✓Hourly PM2.5 up to 17:00 today, and every earlier day in full
✓Lags and rolling means built only from completed days
✓Calendar facts — month, weekday, festival dates
✓Tomorrow's weather forecast from Open-Meteo
✗Today's complete 24-hour mean
✗Tomorrow's actual weather — this is the train/serve gap to watch
✗Anything at all measured tomorrow

Ticket DAF-15 turns this list into a written feature contract, and DAF-12 proves that a random split quietly breaks it.

What counts as success

Asha already has a method, it is free, and on most days it is right. It is the thing to beat.

The persistence baseline

“Tomorrow's PM2.5 will be the same as today's.”

Headline metric — MAE

Mean absolute error in µg/m³. The model must come in at least 10% lower than the baseline's MAE. Anything less and it is not worth running a service for.

Safety metric — recall

Of all the days that truly were Poor or worse (≥ 91 µg/m³), how many did we call? This must not drop below the baseline. A model that lowers MAE by smoothing away the bad days has failed at the only job that matters.

Both numbers are still empty. They get filled in by DAF-17 (the baseline) and DAF-22 (the final test run).

What this is not

Not hourly

One number per day in version one. An hourly forecast is a different problem.

Not other cities

Delhi only. Not other pollutants either — PM2.5 alone.

Not an app

One REST endpoint in one container. No accounts, no mobile app, no multi-day horizon.
