Central Limit Theorem

Why the bell curve keeps appearing, even when the data is nothing like a bell.

1 notebook✍️ 2 handwritten pages

The problem behind this topic

Ishaan rolled one die ten thousand times and plotted the results. Flat — every face equally likely. No bell curve anywhere.

Then he rolled five dice at a time and plotted the average of each throw.

A bell curve appeared, out of flat data.

That is the Central Limit Theorem, and it is why so much of statistics works: you rarely need the population to be normal, only the averages you take from it — and those become normal on their own.

🔑 The one idea

Averages of samples form a normal distribution, no matter what shape the original data had.
